About the foundation

Twelve years of building classrooms that stay open.

We started with one collapsed school roof in a village in Bihar. Today we work across six states, because a good school shouldn't be a matter of luck.

Where it began

A roof that gave way, and a promise that didn't

In 2014, our founder Meera Anand was documenting rural infrastructure when a school roof collapsed overnight in Chandpur village — thankfully during the night, with no children inside. The community asked for help rebuilding it. That single classroom became the first of what is now forty-two schools.

We are not a charity that parachutes in and leaves. Every school we build is handed over to a local council, staffed by local teachers, and visited by our field team every quarter for five years after opening.

Our journey

How we grew, one village at a time

2014

The first classroom

Chandpur village, Bihar — one rebuilt roof, thirty-one children back in class within six weeks.

2017

Teacher fellowship launched

We began training and paying local young adults to teach in their own villages, rather than flying in outside staff.

2020

Mother-tongue libraries

Reading corners in five regional languages, designed with local linguists and illustrators.

2023

Six states, forty schools

Expansion into Odisha and Madhya Pradesh, crossing eighteen thousand enrolled children.

2026

Forty-two schools and counting

A solar-power pilot begins in three schools with unreliable electricity access.

What we stand for

Four principles behind every decision

C

Community-led

We build what a village council asks for, never what looks good on a report.

L

Local employment

Every teacher and builder we hire lives within the community they serve.

T

Full transparency

Every donation is traceable to a specific school, and our accounts are published yearly.

S

Long-term staying

We don't leave at ribbon-cutting — we stay for five years of monitoring.
